# CS-250: Software Development Lifecycle

## Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I think understanding what the user actually needs is one of the most important parts of developing a program. Throughout this course, I learned that it's not just about creating features but making sure those features solve a problem or improve the user's experience. User stories help break down those needs into smaller, more manageable tasks. While working on the SNHU Travel project, I was able to see how user stories provided direction for the development team and helped everyone understand what needed to be accomplished. They also made it easier to identify expectations and determine whether a feature was working as intended.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

Before this course, I mostly thought about software development as following a plan from beginning to end. Learning about Agile and Scrum helped me understand the importance of being flexible and making improvements throughout the process. I like the idea of working in smaller increments because it gives the team opportunities to review progress, receive feedback, and make adjustments before moving forward. In future development work, I would want to incorporate sprint planning, regular communication, and retrospectives. I think these practices help keep projects organized while allowing teams to adjust when priorities or requirements change.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To me, being a good team member means being dependable, communicating, and understanding how your responsibilities affect everyone else's work. One thing that stood out to me throughout this course was how much each Scrum role depends on the others. Even when team members have different responsibilities, everyone is working toward the same goal. I think it's important to be open to feedback, ask questions when something isn't clear, and be willing to help when challenges come up. Being a good team member isn't just about completing your own tasks but also contributing to the team's overall success.
